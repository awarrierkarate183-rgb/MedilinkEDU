Semester curriculum

Four tracks. Twelve modules. Any high school chapter can run this. Titles and one-line descriptions are public on this page. Slide decks, worksheets, and competition packets wait until a chapter approves you on the roster. Login is not live yet. Until it is, ask your chapter leader for curriculum files. Track 4's yearly capstone becomes the basis for that year's Nationals case, so the syllabus and the flagship stay linked.

Track 1

Foundations of Health Economics

How care is paid for, why it costs what it costs, and who is left out. This track gives every member a shared language before they pick a competition. It is not a finance-only elective. Clinical gaps show up here as soon as you ask who cannot pay.

1.1 How Healthcare Gets Paid For

Insurance basics, premiums, deductibles, and employer vs. government coverage. Members should be able to explain how a family actually reaches a clinic, not only how a plan is marketed.

1.2 The Cost of Care

Why healthcare is expensive: administrative costs, drug pricing, and hospital billing. The goal is to see the bill as a system, not as a single shocking number.

1.3 Access and Disparities

Rural care deserts, uninsured populations, and global health gaps. This module is where the clinical lens and the financial lens meet on the same page.

Lab

Build a basic insurance-comparison worksheet for a hypothetical family. Full worksheets wait behind roster approval. This line is the public description of the lab, not the file itself.

Track 2

Health Technology and Systems

Records, data, devices. No coding background required. The test is whether a tool changes access or operations, not whether it looks new. The chapter Technology Lead owns this track inside the chapter.

2.1 Digital Health 101

Electronic health records, telehealth, and patient portals. Members should be able to say what sits between a patient and a clinician before they pitch a new screen.

2.2 Data and AI in Medicine

Diagnostics, imaging, and predictive tools, explained conceptually. This is literacy, not a coding bootcamp. Students still have to ask who is left out of the data.

2.3 Medical Devices and Wearables

How monitoring technology is changing care delivery. A device that patients will not use, or that nobody pays for, fails the other two lenses.

Lab

Wireframe one feature of a telehealth app. Finished product not required. Innovation Challenge later asks for a pitch. This lab is the practice, not the competition.

Track 3

Financial Modeling for Health Ventures

Budgets, funding paths, and why return on investment looks different in health than in a typical consumer business. The chapter Finance / Treasury Lead owns this track. Nationals Cost award work starts here, not in a last-week spreadsheet.

3.1 Reading a Budget

Revenue, expenses, and break-even basics. Members should be able to read a one-page budget and say what happens if volume drops or a grant ends.

3.2 Funding a Health Idea

Grants, investors, and nonprofit vs. for-profit funding paths. A Nationals team that cannot say who pays has not finished the financial lens.

3.3 Measuring Impact and ROI

Why return on investment is measured differently in health than in typical business. Lives, access, and avoided cost sit next to revenue. The worksheet makes that visible.

Lab

Build a one-page budget and funding plan for a mock community health program. Full files wait behind approval. This is the public description of the lab.

Track 4

Applied Capstone and Competition Prep

The yearly capstone becomes that year's Nationals case. That is why this track exists. Packets still use the internal name Care, Cost, Code. About is the page that opens each lens in full. This track is where a chapter practices holding all three on one case, then splits into event-specific prep.

4.1 Applying the Framework

One case run through the three-lens framework. Clinical, financial, and technology on the same page. Packets still say Care, Cost, Code. Do not treat this module as optional if the chapter plans to send a Nationals team.

4.2 Case Analysis Practice

Guided practice on past-style cases. The point is repetition: name the patient, name the payer, name the system, then argue a change that could last.

4.3 Competition-Specific Prep

Pitch prep for Innovation Challenge. Brief-writing for Policy Cup. Poster prep for Research Symposium. Case-team prep for Nationals. The three one-time events have no ladder. Only Nationals climbs Regional to State to National.
