Who we are

Two high school students in Charlotte, North Carolina started MediLink because existing clubs split the work. One club trains clinical skill. Another trains business. A third treats technology as a side interest. Health systems do not work that way. We ask students to hold all three on one case.

01 / Identity

What we are

A student-founded nonprofit network of high school chapters. Members learn how real health systems work: a clinical problem, a money problem, and a technology problem at once. Chapters share one officer structure, one public syllabus, and one competition calendar.

We are not a hospital foundation and not a career-club franchise. The work is chapter meetings, four tracks, twelve modules, and five competitions. Nationals is the yearly flagship. The Apex is the biennial upgrade of that National round.

02 / Direction

Mission and vision

Our Mission

To train the next generation of healthcare problem-solvers by combining clinical understanding, financial reasoning, and technological literacy through chapters, curriculum, and competition.

Our Vision

A nationwide student network where every chapter graduate can walk into a hospital, an insurance company, or a health-tech startup and already understand how those three worlds connect.

03 / Method

The framework

Every module, case, and chapter project runs through three lenses. Open one. The others stay closed so you can read at your own pace. Case packets still use the internal name Care, Cost, Code.

What is the actual health problem, and who is affected?

Name the condition, the patients or communities, and the gap in care. A team that skips this step writes a business plan for a problem it cannot describe. In Nationals, this lens is the Care award: Best Clinical Insight.

Ask who is left out. Rural distance, uninsured families, and delayed diagnosis are clinical facts, not background color.

Who pays for it, what does it cost, and is a solution sustainable?

Insurance, hospital billing, grants, premiums, and unit economics sit here. If the money path fails, the clinical idea does not leave the page. In Nationals, this lens is the Cost award: Best Financial Model.

A chapter Finance Lead owns this lens in meetings: Track 3 labs, budgets, and who actually pays.

How could data, software, or infrastructure change access or delivery?

Records, telehealth, devices, and simple tools count. The test is whether the tech changes the clinical or financial outcome, not whether it looks new. In Nationals, this lens is the Code award: Best Tech Innovation.

A chapter Technology Lead owns this lens: Track 2 modules and any digital project the chapter runs.

Chapters teach this across four tracks and twelve modules. See the public syllabus

04 / Aims

What we aim to achieve

  • Train high school students to apply the three-lens framework to real health-system problems.
  • Grow a nationwide chapter network with a shared officer structure, curriculum, and starter kit.
  • Run four annual competitions plus a biennial Apex, with Nationals as the yearly flagship.
  • Connect members with clinical, finance, and health-tech partners for shadowing, internships, and research.
  • Give students titles, projects, and competition experience that transfer to college and career.
  • Give back to the community through chapter service and outreach.
