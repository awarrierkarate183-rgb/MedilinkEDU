Who we are

Two high school students in Charlotte, North Carolina started MediLink because existing clubs split the work. One club trains clinical skill. Another trains business. A third treats technology as a side interest. Health systems do not work that way. Open a file below.

Identity

A student-founded network of high school chapters.

Click a file for the long version. Mission and vision also live on Goals. If you change one, change both.

Members learn how real health systems work: a clinical problem, a money problem, and a technology problem at once. Chapters share one officer structure, one public syllabus, and one competition calendar. The work is meetings, four tracks, twelve modules, and five competitions.

We do not place students in clinical roles from this website. Shadowing runs through partners and chapter advisors after leadership sets up the relationship. Nationals is the yearly flagship. The Apex is the biennial upgrade of that National round, not a sixth competition with its own case.

Mission

Train the next generation of healthcare problem-solvers by combining clinical understanding, financial reasoning, and technological literacy through chapters, curriculum, and competition.

Public vs packet

This site names the three lenses. Packets still say Care, Cost, Code. Keep that internal name in packets only unless you are quoting them.

A nationwide student network where every chapter graduate can walk into a hospital, an insurance company, or a health-tech startup and already understand how those three worlds connect. Fluency here does not mean a license to practice. It means a student can name the patient, the payer, and the system, and write that down in a case.

That is why the three lenses are not electives. A member who only studies clinic will miss why a good idea never ships. A member who only studies finance will optimize a problem they cannot describe. A member who only studies tech will ship a tool nobody asked for.

Three lenses

Open each lens.

This is the only page that explains them at length. Everywhere else should name them and link here.

Name the condition, the patients or communities, and the gap in care. Who is left out, and why. Rural distance, uninsured families, delayed diagnosis, language access, and wait times are examples of gaps, not a complete list. If you cannot point to a person or a community, you have a theme, not a clinical problem.

Skip this lens and a team writes a business plan for a problem it cannot describe. Packets still use the internal name Care. Teams of 3 to 4 must cover this lens. A Grand Prize still has to hold clinic, money, and tech together.

Insurance, hospital billing, premiums, deductibles, employer coverage, public programs, and grants belong here. If the money path fails, the clinical idea stays on the page. A team that cannot say who writes the check has not finished the case.

The chapter Finance / Treasury Lead owns Track 3 labs, budgets, and finance workshops. Packets still use the internal name Cost. Unit economics in health are not the same as a typical consumer product. Track 3 is built for that difference.

Records, telehealth, devices, or a simple tool. The test is outcome, not novelty. A finished product is not required. Coding background is not required to take Track 2. If the answer is only that the idea is new, it is not yet a technology solution.

The chapter Technology Lead owns Track 2 and digital projects. Packets still say Care, Cost, Code. Innovation Challenge is a separate annual pitch and does not replace this lens on a Nationals case.

What we aim to achieve

The same six aims live on the Goals page. They describe the work, not results already in hand.

  • Train students to apply the three-lens framework to real health-system problems.
  • Grow a nationwide chapter network with a shared officer structure, curriculum, and starter kit.
  • Run four annual competitions plus a biennial Apex, with Nationals as the yearly flagship.
  • Connect members with clinical, finance, and health-tech partners for shadowing, internships, and research.
  • Give students titles, projects, and competition experience that transfer to college and career.
  • Give back through chapter service and outreach.
