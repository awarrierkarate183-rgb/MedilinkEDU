MediLink is a nonprofit network of high school chapters. Members learn how real health systems work: a clinical problem, a money problem, and a technology problem at once. That is the whole method. Chapters do not pick a favorite lane and ignore the other two.

Every chapter uses the same five officer roles, the same public syllabus, and the same competition calendar. The public work of the network is meetings, four tracks, twelve modules, and five competitions. Titles and one-line module descriptions stay on this site. Full lessons wait behind roster approval. Login is not live yet.

What we are not

  • Not a hospital foundation. We do not place students in clinical roles from this website.
  • Not a franchise. Status labels track work already done. They are not marketing badges for sale.
  • Not a public job board. Shadowing and internships run through partners and chapter advisors after leadership sets up the relationship.
  • Not a sixth-case Apex. Nationals is the yearly flagship. The Apex is the biennial enlargement of that National round.

A nationwide student network where every chapter graduate can walk into a hospital, an insurance company, or a health-tech startup and already understand how those three worlds connect. That sentence also lives on Goals. If you change it, change both.

Fluency here does not mean a license to practice, a job offer, or a product already in market. It means a student can name the patient, the payer, and the system, and write that down in a case. A Grand Prize still has to hold clinic, money, and tech together. A poster, a brief, or a pitch that only covers one world is unfinished work.

That is why the three lenses are not electives. A member who only studies clinic will miss why a good idea never ships. A member who only studies finance will optimize a problem they cannot describe. A member who only studies tech will ship a tool nobody asked for.

Three lenses

Open each lens.

This is the only page that explains them at length. Everywhere else should name them and link here.

Name the condition, the patients or communities, and the gap in care. Who is left out, and why. Rural distance, uninsured families, delayed diagnosis, language access, and wait times are examples of gaps, not a complete list. If you cannot point to a person or a community, you have a theme, not a clinical problem.

This lens is not a license to practice. Members do not treat patients from a club meeting. Clinical partners help students name the patient and the gap. Shadowing runs through those partners and chapter advisors after leadership sets up the relationship.

What a finished clinical file includes

  • The condition or unmet need, stated in plain language.
  • The people or community affected, not a slogan about "everyone."
  • The gap: why current care does not reach them, or does not work.
  • What would count as better access or a better outcome, even if the team cannot yet measure it in the field.

Skip this lens and a team writes a business plan for a problem it cannot describe. Packets still use the internal name Care. Teams of 3 to 4 must cover this lens on a Nationals case. A Grand Prize still has to hold clinic, money, and tech together.

Nationals award

Best Clinical Insight. Sponsors can name a track or award. Naming does not change the method judges use.

Where it lives in the chapter

Every officer still owns this lens on a case. Clinical partners and Care-lens judges support it. It is not an elective track you can skip.

Insurance, hospital billing, premiums, deductibles, employer coverage, public programs, and grants belong here. If the money path fails, the clinical idea stays on the page. A team that cannot say who writes the check has not finished the case.

Unit economics in health are not the same as a typical consumer product. A family, an employer, an insurer, and a hospital can all be paying different parts of the same visit. Track 3 is built for that difference. Packets still use the internal name Cost.

What a finished financial file includes

  • Who pays today, and who would pay if the idea shipped.
  • What the idea costs to run, even as a one-page budget.
  • Whether it lasts past a grant, a school year, or a single event.
  • Why return on investment looks different in health than in a typical consumer product.

Chapter owner

The Finance / Treasury Lead owns Track 3 labs, chapter budgets, and finance workshops. Partners from insurers, healthcare finance, consulting, and biotech investors sit here as Cost-lens judges or Track 3 teachers.

What a finished technology file includes

  • What data, software, device, or infrastructure actually changes.
  • Who uses it: patient, family, clinician, or administrator.
  • What access or outcome improves if it works.
  • What is left as a wireframe, a workflow, or a concept, honestly labeled as such.

Nationals award

06 / Aims

What we aim to achieve

The same six aims live on the Goals page. They describe the work, not results already in hand.

  • Train students to apply the three-lens framework to real health-system problems.
  • Grow a nationwide chapter network with a shared officer structure, curriculum, and starter kit.
  • Run four annual competitions plus a biennial Apex, with Nationals as the yearly flagship.
  • Connect members with clinical, finance, and health-tech partners for shadowing, internships, and research.
  • Give students titles, projects, and competition experience that transfer to college and career.
  • Give back through chapter service and outreach.
