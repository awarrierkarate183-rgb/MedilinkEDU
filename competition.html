Compete / Five events

Five competitions. Two structures.

Innovation Challenge, Policy Cup, and Research Symposium each run once a year, with no regional or state qualifier. Nationals is the only event that climbs Regional to State to National. Every other year, that National round becomes the Apex. Dates come from state chapter boards through chapter leaders. This page is the structure, not a calendar.

Yearly flagship

MediLink Nationals

Teams of 3 to 4 take a health-system case from that year's Track 4 capstone.

The yearly flagship. Same case for every team. Curriculum and competition stay connected because the case comes from Track 4.

Who can enter: grades 9-12 in a Flagship-Eligible chapter, or a chapter on that path. Teams of 3 to 4 must cover all three lenses: clinical, financial, and technology.

What a year looks like: a written brief and video pitch, then live regional rounds, then a national final and awards gala. State boards set dates. The intended home of the final is Charlotte. Packets use the internal name Care, Cost, Code.

Awards: Best Clinical Insight, Best Financial Model, Best Tech Innovation, and a Grand Prize for strength across the three-lens framework. Sponsors can name a track or award.

The Nationals ladder

Regional. State. National.

Only Nationals uses this climb. Innovation Challenge, Policy Cup, and Research Symposium do not have regional or state qualifiers.

The year also has three phases that sit on this ladder. Phase 1 is Case Release: a remote brief plus a short video pitch. Phase 2 is Regional Rounds: top teams present live to judges from hospitals, finance firms, and health-tech companies. Phase 3 is National Finals: a live final and awards evening.

Level 1

Regional

First Nationals round, run at or near chapters.

Level 2

State

Top regional teams advance. The state board coordinates this round.

Level 3

National

State qualifiers compete nationally. Live final and awards evening. In an Apex year, this level is the summit.

One annual event

Innovation Challenge

A pitch for an app, device concept, or platform idea. No qualifier rounds.

What it is: students pitch a health-tech idea that addresses a gap in care, access, or operations. Judges score feasibility, impact, and a basic business case. A finished product is not required.

Who can participate: grades 9-12 in an active MediLink chapter. Individual or teams of up to 3.

What to expect: one annual pitch deck and live presentation. No regional or state qualifier. Top finishers earn Apex points on the one-time event schedule.

One annual event

Policy Cup

A brief and debate. Topics come from the state chapter board.

What it is: competitors research a live issue, write a brief, and argue for a concrete reform. Debate and presentation, not a written exam. Topics come from the state chapter board.

Who can participate: grades 9-12 in an active MediLink chapter. Individual or pairs.

What to expect: one annual brief plus live advocacy. No qualifier rounds. Top finishers earn Apex points on the one-time event schedule.

One annual event

Research Symposium

A poster and talk. Lowest barrier of the five.

What it is: students share independent research through a poster and a short presentation. This is the lowest barrier of the five events.

Who can participate: grades 9-12 in an active chapter, working on or finished with research in health economics or health tech.

What to expect: one annual poster display plus an oral presentation. No qualifier rounds. Top finishers earn Apex points on the one-time event schedule.

Biennial culmination

The MediLink Apex

Not a sixth standalone case. Nationals stays the yearly flagship.

Every two years, that cycle's National round becomes a summit for top performers from the other four events. It is not a sixth standalone case. Nationals stays the yearly flagship. In an Apex year the National round is enlarged, and champions from Innovation Challenge, Policy Cup, and Research Symposium are invited.

The summit includes a championship round that pulls from all four annual disciplines, a sponsor networking fair (face time with students, not only a logo on a banner), keynote speakers from medicine, finance, and health-tech, and a grand awards gala.
