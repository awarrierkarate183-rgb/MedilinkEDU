MediLink

We train students to solve healthcare the way real systems are solved.

MediLink is a student-founded high school network started in Charlotte, North Carolina. Members work through chapters, a shared four-track syllabus, and five competitions. The training is built around one idea: a health-system problem is clinical, financial, and technical at the same time. We do not split those into three clubs.

01 / Method

One problem. Three lenses.

A hospital bill, a coverage rule, and a records system sit on the same case. Real health systems do not split those problems across three clubs. A clinical idea that cannot name who pays stays on the page. A finance model that cannot name the patient is a spreadsheet with no problem. A tech demo that does not change access is novelty.

Chapters hold all three from the first meeting. About is the only page that opens each lens in full: what the health problem is, who pays, and how data or infrastructure changes the outcome. Case packets still use the internal name Care, Cost, Code so the public syllabus and Nationals stay linked.

How we train

Every module, chapter project, and competition case is run through the clinical, financial, and technology lenses together. Members do not pick one track and ignore the others. The work is to hold the whole system on one page.

Where it lives

About is the long explanation. Curriculum is the public syllabus. Nationals uses that year's Track 4 capstone as the shared case. If you only need the method, start on About.

02 / Compete

Five events. Two structures.

MediLink runs five competitions, not six. Three of them are one-time annual events: Innovation Challenge, Policy Cup, and Research Symposium. Each of those runs once a year. There is no regional qualifier and no state qualifier. A student or small team enters that event and presents that year.

Nationals is the yearly flagship and the only event that climbs Regional to State to National. Teams of 3 to 4 work the same Track 4 capstone case. Every other year that National round is enlarged into the Apex. The Apex is not a sixth case. Top performers from the other four events are invited into that summit year.

One-time annual

Innovation Challenge is a pitch. Policy Cup is a brief and debate. Research Symposium is a poster and talk. Each is one event per year. Apex points for those events sit on the one-time schedule, not a ladder.

Nationals and Apex

Nationals is the only ladder. Dates come from state chapter boards through chapter leaders. This site is the structure, not a calendar. Full rules live on the Competitions page.

03 / Belong

Chapters earn their status.

Two high school students in Charlotte started MediLink because existing clubs split clinic, business, and tech. A chapter is the local answer: the same five officer roles, the same public syllabus, and the same competition calendar. Founding chapters receive a unique QR code tied to that school's roster. Members do not sign up as a generic user on this website.

Status is earned by work already done. Founding means the chapter is chartered, has officers, and is running meetings. Established means the chapter has run at least one internal event or case challenge and has 10 or more active members. Flagship-Eligible means the chapter has participated in a Nationals regional round and may send teams to Nationals. Those labels are not marketing badges.
